# Security analysis

This is the security overview of the Varuna verification: what is in scope, the conventions, the soundness spine, and, property by property, what Lean proves, what rests on a named floor, and what is still open ([What remains](#what-remains)). The interactive picture is [`proof-map.html`](proof-map.html). Lean links below are relative to the repository root. snarkVM paths refer to the pinned `snarkVM/` submodule (`Varuna.snarkVMPin`). The spec is `protocol-docs/snark/varuna/varuna-spec-prod.tex`.

The development follows [zcash/ironwood](https://github.com/zcash/ironwood): a layered Lean development over Mathlib, a proof map that shows the theorem graph and its remaining holes, and a trust-boundary census so the trusted base is a build-time property.

**Status**, for the item-by-item sections below. **Lean**: kernel-checked here. **Partial**: Lean checks the core; a composed or concrete statement is missing. **Spec**: argued in the spec only. **Excluded**: a category Ironwood also assumes or does not claim (hash = random oracle, algebraic adversary, hardness, byte encodings); named as a floor where it is an assumption. Completeness of the honest V3 batch, and zero knowledge as a count of masks, are Lean; see Completeness and §5.

## What is being verified

The object is the verifier that snarkVM's validators run, `VarunaVersion.V3`. Varuna is Marlin ([CHMMVW19](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1047)): an algebraic holographic proof (AHP) for R1CS with a holographic indexer and a universal, updatable SRS, compiled with the Sonic polynomial commitment on BLS12-377 and made non-interactive with Fiat–Shamir. The AHP is three checks, snarkVM's linear combinations `rowcheck_zerocheck`, `lineval_sumcheck`, and `matrix_sumcheck`. snarkVM batches several circuits, and several instances of each, into one proof, and V3 adds an IOP round to stop adaptive statement selection. Zero knowledge (masking polynomials and hiding commitments) is a mode.

The relation is indexed R1CS. The index $i$ is a circuit's matrices $A, B, C$, the instance $x$ is its public input, with a leading constant $1$, and the witness $w$ is the rest of the assignment $z = (x, w)$. A triple $(i, x, w)$ is in the relation when $Az \circ Bz = Cz$. A batch is in the relation when each of its (circuit, instance) pairs is.

Marlin states each property twice: for the AHP, against any prover, and for the compiled argument, against efficient adversaries. Below, each property is Marlin's definition followed by what this project proves. Knowledge soundness is the main target. Marlin's fifth property, succinctness, is a proof-size count here (§4).

### Completeness

**Marlin.** For every $(i, x, w)$ in the relation, the honest prover makes the verifier accept with probability $1$.

**Proved here.** Each check is complete on its own. With the honest prover's polynomials the rowcheck, lineval, and matrix residuals are identically zero ([`rowcheckResidual_honest`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L128), [`univariateResidual_honest`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L201), [`matrixResidual_honest`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L332)), so the AHP verifier accepts at every challenge ([`accepts_of_residuals_zero`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L393)). With an honest mask every V3 claim is zero ([`v3_honest_claims_zero`](../../../Varuna/Composition.lean#L277)). An honest opening passes the pairing check, hiding or not ([`kzgCheck_honest`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean#L261), [`kzgCheckHiding_honest`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean#L464)).

**Proved here.** One statement for the batch. On a ready batch and a schedule of the verifier's coins, the honest algebraic prover satisfies `V3Batch.Accepts` ([`honestBatch_accepts`](../../../Varuna/Completeness.lean#L493)). Ready ([`InputBatch.Ready`](../../../Varuna/Completeness.lean#L258)) is subdomain division, a mask that sums to zero, and each circuit satisfying R1CS with bounded matrices, `α` outside its constraint domain, and `β` outside its variable domain ([`InputCircuit.Ready`](../../../Varuna/Completeness.lean#L151)). The schedule ([`BatchSchedule`](../../../Varuna/Completeness.lean#L288)) is the transcript: challenges in `S`, squeeze lengths, and the `η`s read off prepare-third. The rowcheck and lineval identities hold at every challenge; the matrix messages use the two domain conditions above. The fingerprint still checks one captured snarkVM batch proof in Lean's form ([Faithfulness to snarkVM](#faithfulness-to-snarkvm)).

### Soundness

**Marlin.** The AHP is $\varepsilon$-sound if, for every $(i, x)$ that has no witness and every prover, the verifier accepts with probability at most $\varepsilon$.

**Proved here.** Probabilities are counts of bad challenges in a finite challenge set $S$. In the AHP, a prover that picks each message after the challenges before it passes on at most $(d_R + d_L + d_M)\,|S|^2$ of the $|S|^3$ challenge triples, where the $d$s are the degrees of the three residuals ([`ahp_error`](../../../Varuna/Probability.lean#L243); degrees computed in [`ahp_error_concrete`](../../../Varuna/Degree.lean#L70)). Batching costs $k/|S|$ for each squeeze of $k$ combiners (§1.3). After Fiat–Shamir, such a prover makes $Q$ queries to a random oracle that answers a repeated query with its earlier answer, and the verifier recomputes every challenge with $V$ queries of its own. At most $(Q+V)\,b\,|S|^{Q+V-1}$ of the $|S|^{Q+V}$ oracle tapes make the V3 verifier accept a batch whose relation fails. With the batch read off the messages by an extractor, that is [`V3Batch.oracle_soundness`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L653). With the batch an algebraic prover represents with its queries, on the tapes with no trapdoor break, it is [`V3Batch.algebraic_soundness`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L319). snarkVM's sponge absorbs the messages but not the earlier challenges. With its queries the oracle is a table on a finite domain $D$ of queries, and at most $(Q+V)\,b\,|S|^{|D|-1}$ of the $|S|^{|D|}$ tables make the verifier accept, the same error ([`V3Batch.sponge_soundness`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L652)). [`V3Batch.algebraic_soundness_concrete`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L415) computes $b$ from the SRS size and the domain sizes. At the sizes of §2.2, with $Q = 2^{64}$ and 252-bit challenges, that is about $2^{-159}$; $V$, one query per squeezed element, is negligible next to $Q$. The batched commitment openings cost about $2^{-102}$ at the same $Q$, the larger term (§2.5). A statement with no witness fails the relation for every batch, so this count bounds Marlin's $\varepsilon$.

**Assumptions.** Those of knowledge soundness, below.

### Knowledge soundness

**Marlin.** There is an efficient extractor that, from any prover the verifier accepts, outputs a witness, except with probability $\varepsilon$. For the AHP the extractor reads the witness off the prover's polynomials. For the compiled argument Marlin works in the algebraic group model: an algebraic adversary outputs each commitment with its representation over the SRS, and that representation is the polynomial.

**Proved here.** The same model. Under the algebraic-adversary floor each commitment comes with its polynomial ([`represent`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L102)). An accepted opening of a wrong value, or of a polynomial over its degree bound, becomes a computed trapdoor break ([`inspectOpening_break`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L182), [`inspectDegree_break`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L325)). After Fiat–Shamir the prover gives, with each query, the batch it represents ([`AlgebraicProver`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L275)). If that batch and its output batch commit to the same messages with different polynomials, two different polynomials take the same value at $\tau$, again a trapdoor break ([`Clash.trapdoorBreak`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L198)). The soundness theorems above are therefore knowledge soundness: their relation is about the extracted assignment $\hat z = \hat x + v_X \hat w$. On a V3 transcript with no bad challenge, [`V3Batch.sound_of_transcript`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L646) concludes that the transcript binds one statement, the mask sum is zero, and each instance's $\hat z$ equals its public input on the input domain and satisfies $Az \circ Bz = Cz$ ([`Holds`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L476)). The tape count under Soundness is the knowledge error. [`V3Endpoint.sound`](../../../Varuna/Endpoint.lean#L254) is the one-proof case. [`knowledgeSoundness`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean#L185) is Marlin's AHP statement, and [`knowledgeSoundness_bls`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean#L246) states it at the BLS12-377 scalar field. The extractor is a plain `def`, and each way it can fail is returned as data (see Conventions).

**Assumptions.** Poseidon is a random oracle, pairings and the SRS trapdoor are hard, the adversary is algebraic, the SRS is honest, byte encodings are faithful, and the verifying key is the index of the real circuit ([`knowledgeSoundness_rests_on_floors`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean#L260)). These are floors, not Lean axioms. The index floor is exactly the hypotheses `hidx*` of [`satisfies_of_rows`](../../../Varuna/Bridge.lean#L96). In the Fiat–Shamir counts of `algebraic_soundness` and `sponge_soundness`, three hypotheses are floors. `hmsgs` is the encoding floor: given the index, the messages determine the sums they carry and the commitments. `idx`, the index a statement names, is the index floor. `hrep` is the algebraic-adversary floor: a query whose messages encode some batch comes with a batch that represents them. The count also takes correct openings, a step not yet proved rather than a floor ([What remains](#what-remains)).

### Zero knowledge

**Marlin.** The AHP is honest-verifier zero knowledge with query bound $b$. A simulator given $(i, x)$ but not $w$ produces the view of an honest verifier that queries each prover polynomial at most $b$ times outside the domain, with the same distribution as the real view. The compiled argument is zero knowledge when the commitment is hiding: a simulator holding the SRS trapdoor produces proofs indistinguishable from real ones.

**Proved here.** Honest-verifier zero knowledge, as a count of masks on a finite field. Query bound $1$: a constant mask sends one opening outside the domain to any chosen value and leaves the domain values unchanged ([`maskAt`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L52), [`masked_eval_at_query`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L61)), so a simulated rowcheck accepts ([`simulateRowcheck_accepts`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L80)). Query bound $n$: at $n$ distinct queries outside the domain, the degree-$< n$ masks hit every answer tuple once ([`maskedAnswers_bijective`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L355)), so every predicate on the answers has the same number of masks for any base polynomial and for zero ([`card_maskedAnswers`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L391)).

The lineval polynomial built from the public input alone equals the real one once the witness is moved into the ZK mask ([`simulateLineval_eq_real`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L122)). The view that does not read the witness is the query answers, that lineval polynomial and its value at $\beta$, a shared rowcheck value, a matrix message, and the constant blinding ([`ZKSimView`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L408), [`simZKView`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L428)). When $\mathrm{lin}\cdot(z_{\mathrm{real}} - z_{\mathrm{sim}})$ has degree less than the lineval mask, one translation of the two masks sends the real view to the simulated view ([`zkProof_translate`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L453)), and every predicate of the view has the same number of real masks and simulator masks ([`card_zkProof`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L478)). The rowcheck entry is that accept value, which the honest residual and the simulated rowcheck both make zero. It is not the quotient $h_0(\alpha)$. The raw opening $w(\beta)$ is not in the view. Equal lineval polynomials with the same blinding are the same hiding commitment ([`hidingCommit_of_translate`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L496), [`commit_hiding_as_blind`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean#L481)), and the simulator's hiding opening checks ([`simulateHidingLineval_accepts`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L153)). Simulation extractability of that opening is a trapdoor break or the represented value ([`simulation_extractable`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L165)). Pairing independence of `g` and `gamma_g` is a hypothesis of that theorem. A distribution on group elements, and unique responses, stay out.

## Conventions

Ironwood’s [formal-verification page](https://zcash.github.io/ironwood/formal-verification.html) is the style guide.

**Breaks as computed data.** A theorem `soundness ∨ ∃-break` is vacuous in a prime-order group (nontrivial discrete-log relations always exist) and for compressing hashes (collisions always exist). Every reduction is a plain `def` that returns the breaking data, with `Prop` certificates attached. For Varuna the breaks are a Schwartz–Zippel root, a polynomial-commitment binding break (two openings of one commitment), a discrete-log / pairing-assumption break extracted from a Sonic-PC forgery, and a random-oracle collision or programming miss. Extractors are `def`s, and `noncomputable` is kept off that path. `inspectResidual` is the exception: it is noncomputable because it inspects Mathlib polynomials.

**Trust discipline.** General theorems rest only on `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. Concrete closed facts may use `native_decide`, and that extension of the trusted base is named on the census. [`TrustBoundary.lean`](../../../Varuna/TrustBoundary.lean) is the census. `assert_axioms` / `assert_computable` in [`AxiomCheck.lean`](../../../Varuna/AxiomCheck.lean) make it a build-time property. CI fails on `sorry` via `lake build --wfail`. Toy-field samples in `Match.lean` / `SpotCheck.lean`, and the captured proof in `Fingerprint.lean`, use kernel `decide`, so the census stays those three axioms. The group-level MSM / pairing assembly is not captured.

**Floors are identifications, not axioms.** Hash-as-RO, pairing-as-hardness, “the verifying key is the index of the real circuit”, and byte-level encodings are floors on the proof map. They do not appear as Lean `axiom`s. A reader of a capstone theorem can see them as explicit hypotheses or as out-of-Lean identification steps ([`knowledgeSoundness_rests_on_floors`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean)).

## The soundness spine

Ironwood’s spine is accepting proof → verifier equation → IPA tree → opening → `SnarkRelation`. Varuna’s spine, reading the Sage verifier and `snarkVM/.../varuna/ahp/ahp.rs`, is:

```
accepting proof
  → Fiat–Shamir challenges          (schedule and adaptive count proved; Poseidon = RO is a floor)
  → Sonic-PC openings               (opening reductions proved; pairing hardness is a floor)
  → AHP linear combinations evaluate to zero
      rowcheck_zerocheck
      lineval_sumcheck
      matrix_sumcheck
  → Az ∘ Bz = Cz
  → R1CS witness
```

[`V3Endpoint.sound`](../../../Varuna/Endpoint.lean) is the composed theorem: a no-break opening of the rowcheck quotient $h_0$, the three matrix sumchecks, and [`v3_chain`](../../../Varuna/Composition.lean#L173) give a zero mask sum and $Az \circ Bz = Cz$ on $R$. [`sound_r1cs`](../../../Varuna/Endpoint.lean) ends at `satisfies`. [`V3Batch.sound`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L361) is the same composition for a batch of circuits and instances, each check one LC over the whole batch, and [`V3Batch.sound_of_transcript`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L646) puts it on a V3 transcript, its checks run on the opened values. [`V3Batch.adaptive_soundness`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L534) bounds, against an adaptive prover, the oracle tapes on which an accepted transcript proves a false statement. [`V3Batch.rbr_soundness`](../../../Varuna/RoundByRound.lean#L288) is the same count through the general round-by-round knowledge soundness definition, of which the V3 batch is an instance. [`V3Batch.oracle_soundness`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L653) is the adaptive bound for a random oracle with memory, with the verifier recomputing the challenges. [`V3Batch.algebraic_soundness`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L319) reads each bad set off the batch the algebraic prover represents with the query instead of an extractor, and [`V3Batch.algebraic_soundness_concrete`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L415) computes its per-query charge from the SRS size and the domain bounds. [`V3Batch.sponge_soundness`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L652) is that count with snarkVM's sponge queries, which carry the messages but not the earlier challenges (§2.2). How the openings, snarkVM's batched pairing check, the batches, and the selector sumcheck enter is §2.3. What stays outside it is [below](#what-remains).

The three checks compose through the lineval. The matrix sumcheck gives each $\sigma_M = \hat M(\alpha, \beta)$ ([`matrix_sumcheck_value`](../../../Varuna/MatrixSumcheck.lean#L208)). The lineval polynomial sums over the variable domain to $\sum_M \eta_M \sigma_M$ ([`sum_linevalPoly`](../../../Varuna/Lineval.lean#L163)), and each target is $\mathrm{LDE}(Mz)(\alpha)$ ([`linevalTarget_eq_mzPoly`](../../../Varuna/Lineval.lean#L115)). [`v3_chain`](../../../Varuna/Composition.lean#L173) combines these with the rowcheck into a zero mask sum and $Az \circ Bz = Cz$ on the constraint domain. The domain identities become the R1CS relation through [`satisfies_of_rows`](../../../Varuna/Bridge.lean#L96) and [`satisfies_iff_zmod`](../../../Varuna/Bridge.lean#L64).

```
  deployed snarkVM verifier          (definition; LC layer fingerprinted)
           │
           ▼
  accepting proof                    (typed predicate, proved iff the Prop)
           │
           ▼
  FS transcript / challenges         (schedule proved; Poseidon = RO is a floor)
           │
           ▼
  PC openings bind the oracles       (algebraic reduction; pairing/SRS floors)
           │
           ▼
  AHP verifier equations             (proved)
     rowcheck ──► lincheck ──► matrix sumcheck
           │
           ▼
  Az ∘ Bz = Cz                       (proved)
           │
           ▼
  R1CS witness                       (proved)
```

## Proof-map statuses

Copied from Ironwood’s proof map. Edge verbs:

- **entails** — B follows from A (and any other incoming arrows)
- **discharges** — A proves away hypothesis B
- **rests on** — B is an assumption A still needs (an in-Lean hypothesis or an out-of-Lean floor)

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `proven` | A named Lean theorem exists; `lake build` checks it |
| `hyp` | In-Lean hypothesis, or a lemma stated but not proved |
| `floor` | Modelling identification, not a Lean axiom |
| `object` | A definition (data or `Prop`), not a theorem |
| `goal` | Advertised capstone |

A node is `proven` only when its `anchor` field names a real declaration. `book/validate-proof-journey.py` checks that every `proven` anchor, and every edge `via`, names a declaration in `Varuna/`. `maskSum` is `v3_chain`: the mask sum is zero.

## Layers

Sorry-free. `lake build --wfail` is the verifier. The numbered sections below say what each security property concludes. This is the file-level inventory those sections draw on.

**R1CS** (`PrimeField.lean`, `R1CS.lean`). Sparse constraints and the Hadamard predicate agree ([`satisfies_iff_hadamard`](../../../Varuna/R1CS.lean#L464)). A multiplication constraint is multiplication ([`mulConstraint_holds_iff`](../../../Varuna/R1CS.lean#L161)). Formatted public inputs prepend the constant-$1$ slot and are admissible iff their length is a power of two ([`formattedPublicInputAdmissible_isPowerOfTwo`](../../../Varuna/Domain.lean#L55)). The toy instance $3 \cdot 5 = 15$ holds over $\mathbb F_{17}$ ([`toy_mul_holds`](../../../Varuna/R1CS.lean#L576)).

**Field, domains, vanishing** (`Field.lean`, `Domain.lean`, `Bridge.lean`). Mathlib `ZMod p` at `v4.33.0`. A multiplicative subgroup of size $2^k$, with $v_H(X) = X^{|H|} - 1$ and $v_H(\alpha) = 0 \leftrightarrow \alpha \in H$, a Lagrange basis, and Schwartz–Zippel ([`schwartzZippel_card`](../../../Varuna/Domain.lean), [`szBadSet`](../../../Varuna/Domain.lean)). The R1CS relation stays on integer residues; [`satisfies_iff_zmod`](../../../Varuna/Bridge.lean#L64) and [`satisfies_of_rows`](../../../Varuna/Bridge.lean#L96) send them to `ZMod p`.

**Indexer** (`Indexer.lean`). Row, column, and value interpolants recover sparse entries on the nonzero domain ([`rowOracle_eval`](../../../Varuna/Indexer.lean#L121) and its siblings). [`holographicEval_at_nodes`](../../../Varuna/Indexer.lean#L85) is the snarkVM identity $M(a,b) = \sum_k \mathrm{val}(k)\, L^R_{\mathrm{row}(k)}(a)\, L^C_{\mathrm{col}(k)}(b)$ at domain nodes.

**AHP, polynomial commitment, Fiat–Shamir, batching.** The three checks, their error, and the batching extractors are §1. Sonic-PC binding, degree bounds, hiding, and simulation extractability are §2.4. The V3 absorb-then-squeeze schedule, including [`prepareThirdEtaSqueeze .V3 = 3`](../../../Varuna/Batching.lean#L386) and the domain separator `VARUNA-2026-V3` ([`v3Init`](../../../Varuna/Statement.lean#L50)), is §3. Forks and collisions are `def`s (`inspectFork`, `inspectCollision`). The first combiner of each family is $1$.

**Public input** (`PublicInput.lean`). snarkVM places the public input on the input subdomain by [`reindexBySubdomain`](../../../Varuna/PublicInput.lean#L34), and witness positions never land on it ([`reindex_witness_mod_ne_zero`](../../../Varuna/PublicInput.lean#L46)). Given canonical generators (`hgen`), $\hat z$ equals the verifier's $\hat x$ at every input position ([`assignment_at_input_position`](../../../Varuna/PublicInput.lean#L73)), which is how the batch relation ties each instance to its public input (§2.3).

**Faithfulness and the capstone.** [`TypedProof.accepts`](../../../Varuna/Match.lean) agrees with the `Prop` accept. Toy-field fixtures accept honest zeros and reject flipped evaluations. The V3 composition (`V3Endpoint.sound`, `sound_of_openings`, `sound_of_combined_matrix`, `matrix_sumcheck_of_selector`, `sound_r1cs`, `V3Batch.sound`, `V3Batch.sound_of_openings`, `V3Batch.sound_of_transcript`, `V3Batch.adaptive_soundness`, `V3Batch.oracle_soundness`, `V3Batch.algebraic_soundness`, `V3Batch.algebraic_soundness_concrete`, `V3Batch.sponge_soundness`, `V3Batch.holds_of_deployedAccepts`) and the Marlin-shaped [`knowledgeSoundness`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean) are §2. [`knowledgeSoundness_bls`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean#L246) restates that statement at `ZMod bls12_377_r`, and `Fingerprint.q_eq_bls12_377_r` shows the captured modulus is that number. [`prime_bls12_377_r`](../../../Varuna/Primality.lean#L116) proves it prime with a Pratt certificate: Lucas's test ([`prime_of_lucas`](../../../Varuna/Primality.lean#L59)) with a witness and the factorization of $r - 1$, and a certificate of its own for each factor above $10^3$. The kernel evaluates the modular powers on numerals. The fingerprint and the source samples are [Faithfulness to snarkVM](#faithfulness-to-snarkvm).

## 1. Soundness of the AHP

### 1.1 The soundness notion (Lean)

The AHP's notion is round-by-round knowledge soundness, because Fiat–Shamir then costs $Q \cdot \varepsilon_{\text{round}}$ (§2.2). Each Varuna check is a polynomial identity at a fresh challenge, so the RBR doomed state is "some residual is nonzero", and the bad challenges are its roots. Lean provides this per check as computed data: [`inspectResidual`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L57), [`inspectResidual_accepts`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L69), and the three extractors [`rowcheck_extract`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L156), [`univariate_extract`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L273), [`matrix_extract`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L371). [`ahp_error`](../../../Varuna/Probability.lean#L243) gives the adaptive union bound across $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$.

### 1.2 Error of the unbatched AHP, including ZK (Lean)

The spec (lines 444–452) gives $\frac{2|R|}{|\mathbb F\setminus R|} + \frac{2|C|}{|\mathbb F\setminus C|} + \frac{3|K|}{|\mathbb F|}$.

Lean proves the shape of the bound over any finite challenge set $S$:

- [`card_filter_inspectResidual_le`](../../../Varuna/Probability.lean#L41): a residual of degree $d$ has at most $d$ bad challenges in $S$.
- [`ahp_error`](../../../Varuna/Probability.lean#L243): an adaptive prover breaks on at most $(d_R + d_L + d_M)\,|S|^2$ of the $|S|^3$ triples.

- [`ahp_error_concrete`](../../../Varuna/Degree.lean#L70): the same bound with the residual degrees computed from degree bounds on the prover's polynomials, by [`natDegree_rowcheckResidual_le`](../../../Varuna/Degree.lean#L30), [`natDegree_univariateResidual_le`](../../../Varuna/Degree.lean#L43), and [`natDegree_matrixResidual_le`](../../../Varuna/Degree.lean#L55). For example, the rowcheck residual has degree at most $\max(\deg z_A + \deg z_B,\ \deg z_C,\ \deg h_0 + |R|)$.

Every count takes an arbitrary finite challenge set $S$; that is where snarkVM's 252-bit AHP challenges enter (`crypto_hash/poseidon.rs:473-476`). Masked witnesses are covered, since the residuals are over arbitrary polynomials. ZK enters through the input bounds, which carry the query bound $b$ (e.g. `deg h_0 ≤ 2|R| + 2b − 2`, `second.rs:66`). The combiner terms are 1.3.

### 1.3 Error of each AHP batching step (Lean)

- **Selectors are indicators of $H_i$ on $H$:** [`selectorPoly_eval_indicator`](../../../Varuna/Selectors.lean#L59).
- **Batched zerocheck:** [`batchedZerocheck_extract`](../../../Varuna/Selectors.lean#L94). A batched rowcheck that is a multiple of $v_H$ gives each circuit's rowcheck on its own domain, or a lucky combination on $H$ as a whole: one that vanishes at every point of $H$ while some claim is live ([`inspectBatchOn`](../../../Varuna/Batching.lean#L189)). No lucky combination at any single point implies the hypothesis ([`inspectBatchOn_eq_none_of_pointwise`](../../../Varuna/Batching.lean#L215)).
- **Batched sumchecks:** [`batchedSumcheck_extract`](../../../Varuna/Selectors.lean#L163) and [`sum_selectorPoly_mul`](../../../Varuna/Selectors.lean#L123). This covers lineval ($\mu, \rho$) batching: per-circuit sums, or a lucky combination.
- **Batched matrix sumcheck:** [`batchedMatrix_extract`](../../../Varuna/MatrixBatch.lean#L117). snarkVM's `matrix_sumcheck` (`ahp.rs:362-400`) is a batched zerocheck on the largest nonzero domain $K$: term $(i, M)$ is the numerator $a - b\,(X g + \sigma)$ of matrix $M$ of circuit $i$, lifted from $K_{M,i}$ by its selector and weighted by $\delta_{M,i}$, and one quotient $h_2$ covers the sum ([`batchedMatrixEval_eq`](../../../Varuna/MatrixBatch.lean#L108)). [`batchedZerocheck_extract`](../../../Varuna/Selectors.lean#L94) gives each numerator on its own $K_{M,i}$, or a lucky $\delta$ combination on $K$ as a whole. [`matrix_sumcheck_value_of_numer`](../../../Varuna/MatrixSumcheck.lean#L152) turns a numerator that vanishes on $K_{M,i}$, with the degree bound on $g_{M,i}$, into $|K_{M,i}|\,\sigma_{M,i} = \hat M_i(\alpha, \beta)$; it needs no quotient for the one matrix.
- **Cost of a lucky combination:** snarkVM draws a round's weights one squeezed element at a time: the instance combiners $\tau_{i,j}$ and then the circuit combiner $\nu_i$ of each circuit (`sample_batch_combiners`, `verifier.rs:50-76`), the $\eta$s (`verifier.rs:193-208`), and the $\delta$s (`verifier.rs:241-246`). Each weight is a product of distinct drawn elements, so the combination of claims is affine in each element with the others fixed ([`coordAffine_scheme`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L219)). Call a prefix of drawn elements live if some completion leaves the combination nonzero somewhere on the domain. At most one next element ends liveness ([`WeightDraw.card_bad_le_one`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L82)), and a combination that is lucky on the whole domain passes such a step ([`WeightDraw.exists_bad_of_lucky`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L110)). Over $k$ drawn elements, at most $k\,|S|^{k-1}$ of the $|S|^k$ draws are lucky, i.e. $k/|S|$ ([`WeightDraw.card_lucky_le`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L132)). A live claim makes the empty prefix live when no two weights are the same monomial ([`exists_weightedSum_scheme_ne_zero`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L266)), so this covers `inspectBatchOn` and `inspectBatch` ([`card_filter_inspectBatchOn_scheme_le`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L329), [`card_filter_inspectBatch_scheme_le`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L342)). The weight schemes are snarkVM's: [`combinerScheme`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L523) is $\nu_i \tau_{i,j}$ in squeeze order (for two circuits of two instances, $1, \tau_{0,1}, \nu_1, \tau_{1,1}\nu_1$: [`schemeWeights_combinerScheme_two_two`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L538)), [`prepareThirdScheme`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L575) is $\nu_i \tau_{i,j} \eta_M$, and [`deltaScheme`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L585) is the $\delta$s; [`combinerScheme_valid`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L566) shows no two instances share a weight. For the batched rowcheck this is [`card_filter_batchedZerocheck_combiners_le`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L646): all of $R$ costs what one point costs, where counting point by point would multiply by $|R|$. For the batched matrix sumcheck of $n$ circuits it is [`card_filter_batchedMatrix_deltas_le`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L683): $3n - 1$ squeezed $\delta$s, lucky on all of $K$ for at most $(3n-1)/|S|$ of the draws. For the batched lineval it is [`card_filter_lineval_weights_le`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L762): [`linevalScheme`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L699) weights the mask sum by $1$ and matrix $M$ of instance $(i, j)$ by $\mu_i \rho_{i,j} \eta_M$, over the third-round combiners and the three $\eta$s ([`schemeWeights_linevalScheme`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L731)). Two claims with weights $[1, \eta]$ are the case $k = 1$, at most one bad $\eta$ ([`card_filter_inspectBatch_pair`](../../../Varuna/Probability.lean#L68), [`card_filter_inspectBatchOn_pair`](../../../Varuna/Probability.lean#L90)).
- **Prepare-third round:** [`alpha_independent_of_prepareThird`](../../../Varuna/Batching.lean#L405) and [`prepareThird_challenge_eq`](../../../Varuna/Batching.lean#L413). $\alpha$ is squeezed before that round; $\eta_A, \eta_B, \eta_C$ are squeezed there ([`prepareThirdEtaSqueeze_V3`](../../../Varuna/Batching.lean#L386)).

### 1.4 Assumptions of AHP soundness (Lean)

AHP soundness is information-theoretic and does not need the AGM. Every theorem here is `assert_axioms`-bounded to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` ([`TrustBoundary.lean`](../../../Varuna/TrustBoundary.lean)). The remaining hypotheses are visible in the statements: primitive roots ([`EvalDomain`](../../../Varuna/Domain.lean#L69)) and degree bounds (`hdeg`), which the PC enforces (2.4).

## 2. Soundness of AHP + PCS compilation

### 2.1 The interactive argument (Lean; algebraic projection)

The compiled object is Marlin's public-coin preprocessing argument of knowledge. [`PreprocessingAHP`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean#L217) is that interaction: a list of rounds, each an absorbed message and a squeezed challenge, plus [`ProofView`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean#L92), the polynomials read off those rounds. [`PreprocessingAHP.sound`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean#L224) requires $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$ to be challenges of the rounds and applies [`knowledgeSoundness`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean#L185). Group elements still come with SRS representations ([`represent`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L102)); the object does not carry group elements itself.

### 2.2 Round-by-round soundness, charged per oracle query (Lean)

The compiled argument is round-by-round knowledge sound, and Fiat–Shamir charges that per oracle query. [`fs_query_charge`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L51): with at most $b$ bad answers per query, at most $Q\,b\,|S|^{Q-1}$ of $|S|^Q$ lazy-oracle tapes let a deterministic adversary hit one. A squeeze of several elements is several queries, each on the prefix extended by the elements already squeezed, as `squeezeN` does ([`squeezeN_before_getElem`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L103), [`elemBefore`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L83)). [`fs_break_count`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L125): an output with a break at any squeezed element is such a hit, if every element was answered on one of its queries ([`OutputFromQueries`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L117)). [`squeezeBad`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L310) is the bad set of each element: Schwartz–Zippel roots at $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, and at the combiner squeezes the elements that end liveness of that squeeze's weight draw (§1.3), at most one each. The first combiners weight the batched rowcheck on all of $R$ ([`rowcheckDraw`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L622)), and the $\delta$s weight the batched matrix sumcheck on all of $K$ ([`deltaDraw`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L662)). [`fs_v2_squeeze_charge`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L358) puts those sets on the query prefixes ([`prefixBad`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L259)) and charges the resulting break count. Squeezed elements enter a prefix as `field` messages, so a query prefix names one squeeze element if no prover message is a lone `field` ([`elemBefore_inj`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L177)). Under that condition, a lucky draw in the output is a break ([`outputBreaks_of_lucky`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L288)); for the rowcheck that is the lucky event of `batchedZerocheck_extract` on snarkVM's weights ([`outputBreaks_of_rowcheck_lucky`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L376)), and for the matrix sumcheck the lucky event of `batchedMatrix_extract` on snarkVM's $\delta$s ([`outputBreaks_of_matrix_lucky`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L391)). For a batch, [`V3Batch.squeezeBad`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L577) is `squeezeBad` with the batch's three residuals and three draws, the prepare-third draw weighting the lineval claims ([`linevalDraw`](../../../Varuna/Combiners.lean#L740)). An output with no break has no residual break and no lucky draw ([`inspectResidual_eq_none_of_no_break`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L405), [`not_lucky_of_no_break`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L421)), which is how [`V3Batch.sound_of_transcript`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L646) consumes it.

**Against an adaptive prover.** The batch's bad sets depend on earlier challenges: the residual at $\alpha$ on the first combiners; at $\beta$ on $\alpha$, the third-round combiners, and the $\eta$s; at $\gamma$ on $\alpha$, $\beta$, and the $\delta$s. `fs_v2_squeeze_charge` charges bad sets fixed before the tape, so it does not cover a prover that picks each message after the challenges before it. [`AdaptiveFS.lean`](../../../Varuna/AdaptiveFS.lean) uses the multi-round Fiat–Shamir encoding instead. Each query carries the statement, then every round's message followed by the elements squeezed after it ([`encodeRounds`](../../../Varuna/AdaptiveFS.lean#L40)), as `squeezeN` already does within a squeeze ([`squeezeN_getElem_rounds`](../../../Varuna/AdaptiveFS.lean#L95)). The V3 init followed by such rounds decodes uniquely ([`v3Init_rounds_inj`](../../../Varuna/AdaptiveFS.lean#L141)), given at least one circuit and one commitment vector per circuit, as `init_sponge` absorbs them. A bad set may then be any function of the statement and the history and still be charged per query ([`fs_rounds_charge`](../../../Varuna/AdaptiveFS.lean#L213)).

[`BatchFS.lean`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean) instantiates this for the batch. On each tape the prover outputs a statement, a transcript, and a batch $B$: the polynomials and sums behind its messages and the values it opens. An extractor reads, from the statement and the messages before each squeeze, what those messages fix of $B$ ([`ExtractsBefore`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L395)). In snarkVM's absorb order, $\hat w$ and the mask come with the first-round message, $h_0$ with the second, the prepare-third sums with the prepare-third message, $g_1, h_1$ with the third, the matrix $g$s and sums with the fourth, and $h_2$ with the fifth ([`V3Batch.upTo`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L197)). This treats the polynomial behind a commitment as a function of the commitment, a binding assumption that `algebraic_soundness` below removes. Each squeeze's bad set reads only data absorbed before it ([`badAt_absorbed`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L323)) and challenges squeezed before it ([`badAt_congr`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L344)), so it is the bad set of its history ([`batchBad_history`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L402)). The history bad set is kept only if it has at most $b$ elements ([`batchBad`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L384)), so the charge needs nothing of the extractor beyond `ExtractsBefore`, and the prover's batch has bad sets that small when its three residuals have degree at most $b$ ([`card_badAt_le`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L336)). [`V3Batch.adaptive_soundness`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L534): if every squeezed element was answered on one of the adversary's $Q$ queries and the prover's batch has residual degrees at most $b \ge 1$ ([`ResidualsBounded`](../../../Varuna/BatchDegree.lean#L325)), at most $Q\,b\,|S|^{Q-1}$ of the $|S|^Q$ tapes yield an output the verifier accepts ([`Accepts`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L447)) while the relation of `sound_of_transcript` fails ([`Holds`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L476)).

[`BatchDegree.lean`](../../../Varuna/BatchDegree.lean) computes $b$. Every polynomial the prover commits to has degree below $D$, the SRS's number of powers, which is the algebraic-adversary floor; every domain is at most $R$, $C$, $X$, $K$ ([`Within`](../../../Varuna/BatchDegree.lean#L222)). The selector $s_{H,H_i}$ has degree $|H| - |H_i|$ ([`natDegree_selectorPoly_le`](../../../Varuna/BatchDegree.lean#L71)), so a claim of degree $|H_i| - 1 + e$ on $H_i$ lifts to degree at most $N - 1 + e$ for any $N$ bounding both domains ([`natDegree_selectorPoly_mul_le`](../../../Varuna/BatchDegree.lean#L83)). The batched rowcheck, lineval, and matrix residuals then have degree at most $\max(2R - 2, D + R - 1)$, $D + C + X - 2$, and $D + K - 1$ ([`natDegree_rowResidual_le`](../../../Varuna/BatchDegree.lean#L252), [`natDegree_linResidual_le`](../../../Varuna/BatchDegree.lean#L270), [`natDegree_matrixResidual_le`](../../../Varuna/BatchDegree.lean#L302)), for any weights ([`Within.residualsBounded`](../../../Varuna/BatchDegree.lean#L331)). The matrix bound uses the deployed $b = |R|\,|C|\,(\alpha\beta - \alpha\,\mathrm{col} - \beta\,\mathrm{row} + \mathrm{row\_col})$ ([`matrixBPoly`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L292)), with the committed [`rowColOracle`](../../../Varuna/Indexer.lean#L117); $a$ and $b$ are then $K$-interpolants ([`natDegree_b_le`](../../../Varuna/BatchDegree.lean#L128)). [`V3Batch.adaptive_soundness_concrete`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L556) is the count at $b = \max(d_R, d_L, d_M, 1)$. With $D = 2^{28}$, $|R|, |C| \le 2^{27}$, $|K| \le 2^{28}$, that is $b = D + K - 1 \approx 2^{29}$, and at $Q = 2^{64}$, $|S| = 2^{252}$ the AHP part after Fiat–Shamir is $Q\,b/|S| \approx 2^{-159}$.

**A random oracle with memory.** `adaptive_soundness` gives a repeated query a fresh tape entry, and it assumes that every squeezed element of the output was answered on one of the prover's queries ([`RoundsFromQueries`](../../../Varuna/AdaptiveFS.lean#L174)). [`MemoOracle.lean`](../../../Varuna/MemoOracle.lean) removes both. The oracle logs its answers: a logged query gets its logged answer, a new query the next tape entry ([`memoRun`](../../../Varuna/MemoOracle.lean#L60)). The new queries are an adversary in the sense of `fs_query_charge`, each a function of the tape entries before it, and every logged answer was given on one of them ([`challengeFromQuery_of_mem`](../../../Varuna/MemoOracle.lean#L108)). After the prover's $Q$ queries the verifier runs ([`withVerifier`](../../../Varuna/MemoOracle.lean#L242)). For each element of each squeeze it asks the statement followed by the element's history ([`nextQuery`](../../../Varuna/MemoOracle.lean#L282)), and it reads the element off the answer ([`fillRounds`](../../../Varuna/MemoOracle.lean#L275)). If it has $V$ queries, at least the number of squeezed elements, every element it reads was answered on one of the $Q + V$ new queries ([`roundsFromQueries_runLog`](../../../Varuna/MemoOracle.lean#L322)), which is `RoundsFromQueries`. In [`V3Batch.oracle_soundness`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L653) the prover is a query strategy whose statement, transcript, and batch are read off its log ([`OracleProver`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L604)), and the challenges are the ones the verifier read ([`OracleProver.chal`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L629)). At most $(Q+V)\,b\,|S|^{Q+V-1}$ of the $|S|^{Q+V}$ tapes give a run on which the verifier accepts while the relation fails. The hypotheses on the prover (a well-formed statement, `ExtractsBefore`, the degree bound) hold on every log. [`V3Batch.oracle_soundness_concrete`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L681) takes $b$ from `Within`. $V$ grows with the number of circuits and instances and is negligible next to $Q = 2^{64}$, so the error stays about $2^{-159}$.

**Algebraic extraction at query time.** `oracle_soundness` still asks for one extractor that maps the messages before each squeeze to the data they fix (`ExtractsBefore`). [`AlgebraicFS.lean`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean) drops it. An algebraic prover gives, with each query, the batch it represents: the polynomials behind the commitments in the query, and what the query carries in the clear ([`AlgebraicProver`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L275)). The bad set of the query is read off that batch ([`repBad`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L235)). It is then a function of the tape entries before the query rather than of the query alone, which `card_hits_le` charges all the same ([`fs_view_rounds_charge`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L48)). A batch is its clear part ([`V3Batch.shape`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L143)) and its committed polynomials ([`V3Batch.committed`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L153)). The two determine it up to the coefficient list of $h_0$ ([`normal_eq_of`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L162)), which no bad set reads ([`badAt_normal`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L184)). Equal messages give the same clear part and the same commitments $p(\tau) \cdot g$ (the hypothesis `hmsgs`). So on the same messages, the batch represented with a query and the output batch have the same bad set ([`repBad_history`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L256)), unless two different polynomials take the same value at $\tau$ ([`Clash`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L194)). Their difference is a nonzero polynomial with root $\tau$ ([`Clash.trapdoorBreak`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L198)). [`V3Batch.algebraic_soundness`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L319): on the tapes with no clash, at most $(Q+V)\,b\,|S|^{Q+V-1}$ of the $|S|^{Q+V}$ tapes give a run on which the verifier accepts while the relation fails. The output batch has the statement's index (`idx`) and the output's messages, and every query whose messages encode some batch comes with a batch that represents them (`hrep`). [`V3Batch.algebraic_soundness_concrete`](../../../Varuna/AlgebraicFS.lean#L415) takes $b$ from `Within`.

**The sponge's queries.** The queries above carry the earlier challenges, the standard multi-round Fiat–Shamir encoding. snarkVM's duplex sponge absorbs only the messages. The sponge state before a squeezed element is the statement, then each message followed by the number of elements squeezed after it ([`spongeRounds`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L258)), and the element is the oracle's answer at that state. This is not a renaming of the history queries. A prover can ask for a later sponge state before it knows the earlier challenges, and the bad set of that query reads challenges answered after it, so a tape that hands out entries in query order does not charge it. [`SpongeFS.lean`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean) makes the oracle a table instead: a list $T \in S^{|D|}$ answers each query of a finite domain $D$ with its own entry, and $0$ off $D$ ([`tableAns`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L127)). The count is over tables. A query decodes as a statement followed by a sponge state in at most one way ([`v3Init_append_inj`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L385)). Its bad set reads the log before it, and the table only at the states of earlier elements ([`spongeBad`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L334), [`spongeBad_congr`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L363)). Changing the entry of a query that was not asked before changes neither the log before it nor its bad set, so at most $b$ of the $|S|$ values of that entry are bad ([`card_resample_le`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L56)). At most $n\,b\,|S|^{|D|-1}$ tables then let one of the first $n$ queries land in its bad set, whatever order the queries come in ([`table_charge`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L190)). [`V3Batch.sponge_soundness`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L652) is `algebraic_soundness` with these queries. The algebraic prover makes $Q$ queries, and `hrep` reads a query as a statement followed by a sponge state. The verifier's challenges are the table's answers at its sponge states ([`spongeChal`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L450)), which $D$ holds on every table (`hD`), for instance all sponge states up to the longest statement. On the tables with no clash ([`SpongeClashes`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L515)), at most $(Q+V)\,b\,|S|^{|D|-1}$ of the $|S|^{|D|}$ give an output the verifier accepts while the relation fails: the error is again $(Q+V)\,b/|S|$. [`V3Batch.sponge_soundness_concrete`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L688) takes $b$ from `Within`.

**Which round-by-round notion.** [`RBRKnowledge`](../../../Varuna/RoundByRound.lean#L107) is the state-function definition of [CCHLRRW19](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/1004) with the extractor of round-by-round knowledge soundness of [CMS19](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/834), counted over $S$, every squeezed element a round. A state function marks histories doomed. The empty history is doomed, and a prover message keeps a history doomed. From a doomed history whose extraction fails, at most $b$ elements of $S$ lead to one that is not doomed, and the verifier rejects doomed transcripts. The elements that leave a doomed history are its bad set ([`RBRKnowledge.bad`](../../../Varuna/RoundByRound.lean#L130)), a function of the statement and the history. [`RBRKnowledge.fs_charge`](../../../Varuna/RoundByRound.lean#L181) is Fiat–Shamir for any such verifier: at most $Q\,b\,|S|^{Q-1}$ tapes give an accepted output from none of whose histories the extractor reads a witness. [`V3Batch.rbrKnowledge`](../../../Varuna/RoundByRound.lean#L249) is the V3 batch as an instance. A history is doomed when no element so far extracts a witness or lands in its batch bad set (`batchBad`). The extractor reads the batch off a history's messages. The relation is fixed by the first message ([`holds_absorbed`](../../../Varuna/RoundByRound.lean#L213)), so the extractor is straight-line, the simple case of CMS19. An accepted transcript with no element in its bad set satisfies the relation ([`holds_of_accepts`](../../../Varuna/BatchFS.lean#L485)), so a doomed one is rejected. [`V3Batch.rbr_soundness`](../../../Varuna/RoundByRound.lean#L288) is the count the instance gives. Round-by-round soundness implies state-restoration soundness ([Holmgren19](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1261)); `fs_rounds_charge` is that step in counting form, charging a prover that tries many histories once per query. One bound $b$ for every round loses nothing after Fiat–Shamir: an adversary can spend all $Q$ queries on the round with the largest bad set.

### 2.3 From openings to the AHP checks (Lean)

Under the algebraic restriction, every PC step either gives the polynomial fact AHP soundness uses or a computed trapdoor break:

- an accepted opening with a wrong value: [`inspectOpening_break`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L182)
- a violated degree bound: [`inspectDegree_break`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L325)
- a batched opening: [`batchedOpening_extract`](../../../Varuna/OpeningBatch.lean#L61) per point, [`acrossPoints_extract`](../../../Varuna/OpeningBatch.lean#L90) across points

[`V3Endpoint.sound`](../../../Varuna/Endpoint.lean#L254) composes the opening reduction with the AHP. A no-break opening of the rowcheck quotient $h_0$ ([`value_correct_of_inspect_none`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L207)), the three matrix sumchecks with their degree bounds ([`matrix_sumcheck_value`](../../../Varuna/MatrixSumcheck.lean#L208)), and [`v3_chain`](../../../Varuna/Composition.lean#L173) give a zero mask sum and $Az \circ Bz = Cz$ on $R$. Each matrix has its own nonzero domain. The $\gamma$ check is `matrixEval` $= 0$ plus [`inspectResidual`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L57), which [`inspectResidual_accepts`](../../../Varuna/AHP.lean#L69) turns into the residual identity [`matrix_sumcheck_value`](../../../Varuna/MatrixSumcheck.lean#L208) consumes. [`sound_of_openings`](../../../Varuna/Endpoint.lean#L331) does the same for $\hat z$, $h_1$, $g_1$, and the three matrix witnesses: a no-break opening plus the scalar check the verifier runs is the polynomial check. [`sound_of_combined_matrix`](../../../Varuna/Endpoint.lean#L407) replaces the three $\gamma$ checks by one $\delta$-combination ([`inspectBatch_accepts`](../../../Varuna/Batching.lean#L177)). [`matrix_sumcheck_of_selector`](../../../Varuna/Endpoint.lean#L459) turns a selector-batched sum on a common domain ([`batchedSumcheck_extract`](../../../Varuna/Selectors.lean#L163)) into $|K|\sigma = \hat M(\alpha,\beta)$ via [`matrix_sumcheck_value_of_sum`](../../../Varuna/MatrixSumcheck.lean#L188). [`V3Batch.sound`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L361) is the endpoint for a batch as snarkVM runs it: several circuits ([`BatchCircuit`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L95)), several instances of each, and each of the three checks one LC over all of them, lifted to the largest domain by selectors, with one quotient. The matrix LC, accepting at $\gamma$ with no residual root and no lucky $\delta$s, gives every circuit's $|K_{M,i}|\,\sigma_{M,i} = \hat M_i(\alpha, \beta)$ ([`batchedMatrix_extract`](../../../Varuna/MatrixBatch.lean#L117)). With those, the lineval LC gives a zero mask sum and each instance's $\sigma_{M,i,j}$ as its lineval target, unless its weights $1, \mu_i \rho_{i,j} \eta_M$ are lucky on the claims ([`linClaims`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L288)). With those, the rowcheck LC and the $h_0$ opening give each instance's rows on its own $R_i$, unless the $\nu_i \tau_{i,j}$ are lucky on $R$ ([`batchedZerocheck_extract`](../../../Varuna/Selectors.lean#L94)). [`V3Batch.sound_of_openings`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L541) runs the lineval and matrix LCs on the values the verifier reads: the opened $s(\beta)$, each $\hat w_{i,j}(\beta)$, $h_1(\beta)$, $g_1(\beta)$, each $g_{M,i}(\gamma)$, and $h_2(\gamma)$, with $\hat z_{i,j}(\beta) = \hat x_{i,j}(\beta) + v_{X_i}(\beta)\,\hat w_{i,j}(\beta)$ assembled from the public input. An opening with no break ([`OpenedAt`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L64)) is the polynomial's value, so these are the LCs `V3Batch.sound` consumes. [`V3Batch.sound_of_transcript`](../../../Varuna/BatchEndpoint.lean#L646) puts this on a V3 transcript. The challenges are its squeezes and the weights are read off them with snarkVM's schemes. The six no-break hypotheses become one: no squeezed element is in its bad set. The init absorbs the batch's public inputs, so the relation holds for the one statement the transcript binds ([`v3Init_injective`](../../../Varuna/Statement.lean#L91)), with each $\hat z$ equal to its public input on the input domain ([`assignment_at_input_position`](../../../Varuna/PublicInput.lean#L73)). [`knowledgeSoundness_bls`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean#L246) restates the Marlin capstone at `ZMod bls12_377_r`. [`prime_bls12_377_r`](../../../Varuna/Primality.lean#L116) proves `bls12_377_r` prime with a Pratt certificate. [`Fingerprint.q_eq_bls12_377_r`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean) shows the captured modulus is that number.

**snarkVM's batch check.** The deployed verifier opens no polynomial on its own, and never sees $h_0(\alpha)$, $s(\beta)$, $\hat w(\beta)$, $h_1(\beta)$, or $h_2(\gamma)$. Its query set is the rowcheck LC at $\alpha$, $g_1$ and the lineval LC at $\beta$, and every circuit's $g_A, g_B, g_C$ and the matrix LC at $\gamma$, each LC opened to $0$ (`messages.rs:100-140`, `ahp.rs:179-404`). `batch_check` (`sonic_pc/mod.rs:347-420`) combines the openings at each point with one challenge $\xi$ per polynomial, and the points with randomizers $1, r_1, r_2$, in one pairing product. Against the algebraic representations that product reads $\sum_j r_j D_j(\tau) = 0$, with $D_j = \sum_i \xi_{j,i} p_{j,i} - \sum_i \xi_{j,i} v_{j,i} - (X - z_j)\,q_j$ the KZG defect at point $j$ ([`PointOpening.defect`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L55)). Unless the $r$ are lucky, every $D_j(\tau)$ is zero. A nonzero $D_j$ with root $\tau$ is a trapdoor break ([`PCBreak.trapdoorBreak`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L86)). $D_j = 0$ makes $\sum_i \xi_{j,i}\,(p_{j,i}(z_j) - v_{j,i})$ zero ([`eval_defect_z`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L63)), so unless the $\xi_j$ are lucky every claimed value is its polynomial's value ([`batchCheck_extract`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L98)). The three LCs are polynomials built, as snarkVM builds them, with the claimed $g_1(\beta)$ and $g_{M,i}(\gamma)$ ([`V3Batch.rowLC`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L156), [`linLC`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L164), [`matLC`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L176)). Once those claims are correct, their values at the points are the checks `sound_of_transcript` runs ([`eval_linLC`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L201), [`eval_matLC`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L207)). [`DeployedAccepts`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L226) is `Accepts` with the batch check over these points in place of correct openings and the three LC equations. [`V3Batch.holds_of_deployedAccepts`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L253): with no squeezed element in its bad set, no lucky combination ([`PCLucky`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L75)), and no trapdoor break ([`PCBreak`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L81)), the relation holds. The degree-bound shifts and the hiding term of the pairing product are group-level assembly outside this reading; the degree bound of $g_1$ stays a hypothesis, as in `Accepts`.

### 2.4 Properties of SonicPCS (Lean)

- **Extractability:** from the algebraic restriction, named as the floor `algebraicAdversary` ([`ModellingFloor`](../../../Varuna/Match.lean#L34)).
- **Evaluation binding:** [`inspectOpening_break`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L182) and [`pairingBreak_of_double_opening`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean#L354); hardness is the `pairingHardness` floor.
- **Degree bounds:** [`inspectDegree_break`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L325). [`natDegree_X_mul_add_C_lt`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L432) turns $\deg g_1 \le |C|-2$ (`third.rs:60`) into `hdeg`.
- **Hiding** (ZK only): [`kzgCheckHiding_honest`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean#L464) is the snarkVM check `e(C − v g − random_v gamma_g, h) = e(w, βh − z h)`. A constant blinding is the shift `ρ · gamma_g` on top of the non-hiding commitment ([`commit_hiding_as_blind`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean#L481), [`commit_blind_shift`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean#L487)), and a nonzero `gamma_g` makes that scalar unique ([`commit_const_blind_injective`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean#L494)). With pairing-independent generators, equal hiding commitments agree on both `p(β)` and `r(β)` ([`commit_hiding_binding`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean#L512)). That independence is a hypothesis, not a proved hardness statement. On a finite field, predicates of the masks and this scalar blinding have the same number of preimages for the real view and the witness-free view ([`card_zkProof`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L478)). That is a count of field elements, not a distribution on the group.
- **Simulation extractability:** an accepted algebraic hiding opening is a trapdoor break or the represented value ([`hidingOpening_extract`](../../../Varuna/Algebraic.lean#L412)). The simulator's hiding opening of the masked lineval checks, and a fresh polynomial is extracted the same way ([`simulation_extractable`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L165)). Padding still makes proofs non-unique (`varuna-padding.tex:256`), so this is not the unique-response property of [WM], and it is one opening rather than the whole SNARK.

### 2.5 Loss from batching SonicPCS openings (Lean)

Both levels reduce to a lucky combination: [`batchedOpening_extract`](../../../Varuna/OpeningBatch.lean#L61) and [`acrossPoints_extract`](../../../Varuna/OpeningBatch.lean#L90). With one free 168-bit challenge per combination (`sonic_pc/mod.rs:280, 373-414`), each costs at most $1/|S|$ ([`card_filter_linear_le_one`](../../../Varuna/Probability.lean#L59)), so the three points cost about $2^{-166}$. [`batchCheck_extract`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L98) is both levels on snarkVM's check, one challenge per polynomial at each point, and §2.3 composes it with the AHP checks. The randomizers are transcript-derived, so [`fs_query_charge`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L51) multiplies by $Q$: $Q = 2^{64}$ gives about $2^{-102}$. The target security level should be fixed together with $Q$.

## 3. Soundness of the Fiat–Shamir transform (Lean; Poseidon = RO excluded)

- **Schedule:** absorb then squeeze, with each challenge a function of the prefix before that squeeze ([`challenge_eq_ro`](../../../Varuna/FiatShamir.lean#L162)), and each further element of a squeeze a function of that prefix and the elements before it ([`squeezeN_before_getElem`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L103)). V3 squeezes $\alpha$ alone in the second round and $\eta_A, \eta_B, \eta_C$ in prepare-third ([`sample_v3_second_round_squeeze`](../../../Varuna/SpotCheck.lean#L72), [`sample_v3_prepareThird_eta_squeezes`](../../../Varuna/SpotCheck.lean#L67)).
- **Statement binding:** [`v3Init`](../../../Varuna/Statement.lean#L50) models `init_sponge` with domain separator `VARUNA-2026-V3`. [`v3Init_injective`](../../../Varuna/Statement.lean#L91): the initial transcript determines the public inputs and the commitments.
- **Query charging:** [`fs_query_charge`](../../../Varuna/FSBound.lean#L51); against an adaptive prover, with queries that carry the history, [`fs_rounds_charge`](../../../Varuna/AdaptiveFS.lean#L213). The oracle remembers its answers and the verifier's own queries recompute the challenges ([`roundsFromQueries_runLog`](../../../Varuna/MemoOracle.lean#L322), §2.2). With the sponge's queries, which carry only the messages, the oracle is a table, and the entry of each new query is resampled ([`table_charge`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L190), §2.2).
- **Poseidon = RO:** a floor. The model is the sponge as a random oracle on its states: the statement, the messages, and the number of elements squeezed after each ([`spongeRounds`](../../../Varuna/SpongeFS.lean#L258)).

## 4. Succinctness (Lean count; asymptotics from the spec)

[`ProofShape`](../../../Varuna/ProofSize.lean#L30) counts snarkVM's `Proof` (`data_structures/proof.rs`). For $i$ circuits and $J$ instances in ZK mode, that is $J + 3i + 8$ in $\mathbb G_1$ and $1 + 6i + 3J + 3$ in $\mathbb F$ ([`g1_eq`](../../../Varuna/ProofSize.lean#L63), [`fr_eq`](../../../Varuna/ProofSize.lean#L68)).

The spec's $9\,\mathbb G_1 + 10\,\mathbb F$ omits the three KZG witnesses and the `random_v` values ([`spec_single_proof`](../../../Varuna/ProofSize.lean#L74)). Its batch $\mathbb F$ count $1 + 9i$ is right only when $J = i$ ([`spec_batch_scalars_iff`](../../../Varuna/ProofSize.lean#L86)). Verifier time (2 pairings plus $O(\sum |x| + \log |R_{\max}|)$) is the spec's; Lean has no cost model.

## 5. Zero knowledge (Lean)

The AHP simulator is honest-verifier. Query bound $1$ is [`maskAt`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L52), the constant mask that sends one opening outside the domain to any chosen field element and leaves the domain values unchanged ([`masked_eval_at_query`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L61), [`masked_agrees_on_domain`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L56)). [`simulateRowcheck`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L76) then makes the rowcheck accept at that challenge ([`simulateRowcheck_accepts`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L80)). Query bound $n$ is [`maskedAnswers_bijective`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L355): degree-$< n$ masks, at $n$ distinct points outside the domain, hit every answer tuple once. [`card_maskedAnswers`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L391) is the same number of such masks for every base polynomial.

[`simulateLineval`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L116) builds the lineval polynomial from the public input and a mask, with no witness. [`simulateLineval_eq_real`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L122) moves a real witness into the ZK mask, and [`simulateLineval_witness`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L135) shows the honest sumcheck witness agrees. [`simulateLineval_accepts`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L146) is the simulated check. In non-ZK mode the mask is dropped ([`linevalPolyEta_nonZK_ignores_mask`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L96)), so the witness cannot be moved. When that witness term has degree less than the mask length, [`zkProof_translate`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L453) sends the real view to [`simZKView`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L428), and [`card_zkProof`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L478) counts the masks. The view's rowcheck entry is the accept value, not $h_0(\alpha)$, and $w(\beta)$ is absent.

The hiding commitment of that polynomial is the non-hiding commitment plus a constant blinding along `gamma_g`. [`simulateHidingLineval_accepts`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L153) is the honest `random_v` opening. [`hidingCommit_of_translate`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L496) keeps that commitment fixed when the lineval mask absorbs the witness and the blinding does not change. [`simulation_extractable`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L165) says that opening checks, and that a fresh algebraic opening of a different polynomial is a trapdoor break or the represented value.

## Faithfulness to snarkVM

The theorems are about Lean's model of the verifier. [`Fingerprint.lean`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean) ties that model to the deployed code: it re-checks one captured snarkVM V3 hiding-mode batch proof over two circuits with two instances each ([`batch_shape`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L260); [`fixtures/fingerprint`](../../../fixtures/fingerprint/PROVENANCE.md), from the pinned tree with test-only instrumentation). The verifier sees only combined openings, so the capture is on the prover side, re-assembled in the verifier's LC shape. Kernel `decide` over the BLS12-377 scalar field checks three things:

- every coefficient snarkVM assembles equals Lean's formula, with circuits and instances combined by `weightedSum` ([`matrix_coeffs`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L297), [`lineval_coeffs`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L286), [`rowcheck_coeffs`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L280));
- each LC vanishes, both as snarkVM assembled it and in Lean's batched scalar form ([`matrix_vanishes`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L331), [`matrix_model`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L341));
- the scalar forms are the model ([`eval_selectorBatch`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L82), [`matrixTerm_eq_scalar`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L90)).

The two circuits differ on every domain, so each batching ingredient is exercised. The first combiner of each family is $1$ and the rest are squeezed, with fresh third-round combiners ([`first_combiners_eq_one`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L348), [`later_combiners_squeezed`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L355)). The selector scale is non-trivial at $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$ ([`selectors_scaled`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L364)), and $v_{X_i}(\beta)$ is per circuit ([`input_domains_differ`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L370)). The fixture also shows $\mathrm{row}(\gamma)\,\mathrm{col}(\gamma) \ne \mathrm{row\_col}(\gamma)$ in both circuits ([`product_form_differs`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L375)), so a product-form $b = |R|\,|C|\,(\alpha - \mathrm{row})(\beta - \mathrm{col})$ would agree with the deployed one only on $K$. `matrixBPoly` uses the committed $\mathrm{row\_col}$, so its value at $\gamma$ is the deployed one ([`matrixTerm_eq_scalar`](../../../Varuna/Fingerprint.lean#L90)). [`SpotCheck.lean`](../../../Varuna/SpotCheck.lean) samples the source. The fixture has field elements only, so the group-level MSM / pairing assembly is outside Lean, and byte encodings stay a floor. Sage proofs are not captured: the Sage implementation is a readable single-circuit PIOP, and snarkVM is the verifier of record.

## What remains

`V3Batch.sponge_soundness` counts the oracle tables on which an algebraic prover, querying the sponge's states, gets the AHP checks to accept on correct openings while the relation fails, with no trapdoor break among its representations. Two steps between that count and the deployed verifier are not proved. Each is a hypothesis or a modelling choice visible in the statement, not an axiom.

**The batch check's challenges are not counted.** `V3Batch.Accepts`, which the count takes, includes that the opened $h_0(\alpha)$, and every value in `Openings`, is its polynomial's evaluation. The deployed verifier checks one batched pairing equation instead. [`V3Batch.holds_of_deployedAccepts`](../../../Varuna/BatchCheck.lean#L253) is the field-level step: that equation, with no lucky combination and no trapdoor break, gives the relation (§2.3). The lucky combinations are not counted. The squeezes of the $\xi$ and the $r$ are not rounds of the counted transcript, and the $2^{-102}$ of §2.5 is added by hand.

**Faithfulness past the LC layer.** The fingerprint covers zero-eval LC coefficients of one captured batch proof (two circuits, two instances each). The group-level MSM / pairing assembly, byte encodings, and Sage proofs are outside that capture ([Faithfulness to snarkVM](#faithfulness-to-snarkvm)).

**Named floors.** Poseidon = RO, pairing / trapdoor hardness, algebraic adversary, SRS, encodings, and index = circuit. Commitment hiding is the constant shift $\rho \cdot \mathtt{gamma\_g}$ ([`commit_hiding_as_blind`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean), [`commit_blind_shift`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean)) together with binding of both scalars ([`commit_hiding_binding`](../../../Varuna/SonicPC.lean)). Simulation extractability of one hiding opening is [`simulation_extractable`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L165). The same number of masks for the real view and the witness-free view is [`card_zkProof`](../../../Varuna/ZK.lean#L478), a count of field elements. Unique responses and a distribution over the group stay out: padding makes proofs non-unique, and pairing independence of the two generators is a hypothesis of the hiding binding. [`PreprocessingAHP`](../../../Varuna/Soundness.lean) is the public-coin interaction in the algebraic projection; it carries the represented polynomials, not group elements.
